What the reviews show

  1. The rating you see depends on the country you view it from. Play shows each country its own figure: {% for c in m.countries %}{{ c.country|country }} {{ c.score }}{% if not loop.last %}, {% endif %}{% endfor %}. A customer or colleague outside India sees a lower rating than the team in Bengaluru does.
  2. This is not a recent drop. {{ rv.last_365.pct_low }}% of written reviews in the last 12 months were one or two-star, against {{ rv.lifetime.pct_low }}% over the app's life. No single release is provably worse than the rest.
  3. It is mostly the app, not the cabs. Of low-star reviews that name a problem, {{ s.owner_app_pct }}% describe something product or engineering would fix. {{ s.owner_ops_pct }}% are about late cabs, routes or drivers.
  4. Getting into the app is the largest cluster. Login OTP not arriving, repeated logouts, the app not opening and slowness are {{ s.access_share_pct }}% of low-star reviews.
  5. One problem is growing. “{{ s.recent_top.id|theme }}” is {{ s.recent_top.recent_share_pct }}% of low-star reviews in the last 90 days, up from {{ s.recent_top.earlier_share_pct }}% before.
  6. There is no single fix. The largest named problem is worth about {{ s.top_theme_lift|signed }} on the Indian rating if fully solved; every named problem together, about {{ s.named_lift|signed }}. So the plan below is a ranked set, not one feature.

What I need from MoveInSync to go further

Everything here comes from public data. Four internal sources would turn the estimates into measurements:

  • Play Console access: ratings by country, app version and device, and Android vitals (crash and ANR rates). Public data cannot show how many ratings each country has, or which release crashed.
  • OTP logs for one week: requested, sent, delivered, verified. This separates delivery failures from unregistered users (ticket R5).
  • Support tickets and the trip-feedback configuration per customer: to check whether the forced rating is a product default or a customer setting (R3), and to match complaints to reviews.
  • Trip punctuality by customer and vendor: to tell an inaccurate ETA from a late cab (R8, R9).

When a complaint fits no theme

The themes start as a written list, so that this month's shares can be compared with last month's. A complaint that fits none is not forced into the nearest one: it is labelled “other” with a short note saying what it is. When {{ cfg.alerts.new_theme_min_reviews }} reviews share a note, that note becomes a theme by itself: it gets its own line in the chart and tables, marked “new”, with no one having to add it. The digest flags it so a person can give it a proper definition, merge it, or leave it. A second flag goes up if unthemed complaints pass {{ cfg.alerts.other_share_alert }}% of recent low-star reviews, which would mean the list is going out of date.

In production

Scraping the public page is right for an outside analysis and wrong for a product team that owns the app. Inside MoveInSync the same pipeline should read from the Play Developer Reviews API and Play Console exports, which add ratings without text, per-country counts, device and version data, and crash rates. Only the pull stage changes; cleaning, labelling, metrics and the report stay as they are.

Method and limits

How this was produced

  1. Pull. Every public review of the employee app on Google Play ({{ rv.pulled|commas }}, back to 2014), the rating shown on {{ m.countries|length }} country listings, and the {{ m.labelling.ios_low }} most recent low-star reviews Apple's public feed exposes for the iOS app. Reviewer names are not stored.
  2. Check. Duplicates, impossible scores and dates, and coverage against the review count Play itself reports.
  3. Label. {{ m.labelling.labelled|commas }} reviews of three stars or fewer from the last 12 months, each given a main complaint from {{ m.themes|rejectattr('provisional')|list|length }} themes, an owner, a safety flag and an “after an update” flag. The themes were drafted from reading the reviews, not decided beforehand. Labelling was done by Claude, an AI model, working to written rules in the repository.
  4. Measure. Shares, trends, reply rates and an estimate of each theme's effect on the rating.
  5. Recommend. Tickets written from the evidence and checked against MoveInSync's public help center and Google's guidance on review prompts.

Tools: Python; the open-source google-play-scraper library; Apple's public review feed; Claude Code for writing the pipeline and labelling reviews; pytest for the parts where a silent error would put a wrong number on this page.

What this cannot show

  • Public data only. No crash rates, no ratings by version or device, and no count of ratings per country.
  • Reviewers are not a sample of users. People who write reviews are a small and unhappier group. The themes explain why low ratings happen; they do not measure how many employees are affected.
  • Reviews cannot be split by country. Play returns the same review list everywhere, so the lower ratings abroad cannot be traced to their own complaints from outside.
  • Rating effects are estimates. They assume low raters who write nothing have the same complaints as those who do, and they describe where the average settles, not how fast. Play also weights recent ratings in a way it does not publish.
  • {{ s.no_detail_pct }}% of low-star reviews give no reason. Replying to them (R1) is the only way to learn it.
  • I could not use the app. It needs an employer account, so no complaint was reproduced first-hand.
    • {% if m.validation and cfg.check_set.labelled_by != 'human' %}
  • The check on the labels is not fully independent. {% if cfg.check_set.human_reviewed %}The check set was labelled by a second AI session and then reviewed by a person, which is weaker than a person labelling it blind.{% else %}The agreement figures compare two AI sessions, so they cannot catch an error both share.{% endif %}

Questions I would ask in the first week

  • Product: Which rating are we judged on, the Indian listing or the one a customer abroad sees? What triggers the Play review request today? Is mandatory trip feedback our default or a customer's choice?
  • Engineering: What check produces the “not downloaded from Play Store” message, and when did it ship? What are the crash-free rates for the last five releases? Are releases staged?
  • Support: Who owns the Play review inbox? How many OTP tickets arrive each week, and how do they resolve?
  • Operations: Which customers and vendors account for most late-cab and no-show complaints?
  • A customer's transport desk: What do employees ask you that the app should have told them?
